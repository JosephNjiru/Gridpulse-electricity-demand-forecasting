The question

Can a simple, auditable benchmark expose where demand patterns hold and where they break?

GridPulse treats operational demand as a market-specific measurement contract rather than a generic time series. It validates schemas, cadence, ordering and provenance before a forecast is scored. The local project then compares previous-day and previous-week seasonal-naive forecasts with a base-R ARIMA comparison.

This website presents the software design and a safe synthetic interaction. Empirical development results remain local while source-rights review is incomplete.

Data and methods

Native contracts, explicit boundaries

01

Validate

NEM archives are checked for schema, safe archive members, five expected regions, duplicate keys, 30-minute cadence, source ordering and negative observations.

02

Construct

Development folds use 336 half-hour warm-up intervals, 48 forecast intervals and a 336-interval weekly stride. The final 672 intervals per region remain reserved and unscored.

03

Compare

MAE, RMSE, sMAPE, WAPE, MASE, RMSSE and bias retain their eligibility counts. MAPE uses strictly positive actuals only.

04

Interpret

The study is a retrospective source-order hindcast. Source timestamps are retained verbatim; public issue-time availability and universal UTC conversion are not claimed.

What the local project demonstrates

Fixture tests, strict archive validation, a shared Python/R origin manifest, local MLflow tracking, PySpark parity and a dependency-free loopback dashboard.

What remains separate

Delta Lake, sparklyr, Docker, Terraform, Databricks and remote deployment are optional extensions, not represented as executed public services.

About the project

Built for careful technical conversation.

GridPulse is maintained by Joseph Njiru as a portfolio project for practical electricity analytics, data engineering and reproducible research discussion.

The repository contains the software and fixture tests needed to inspect the implementation. Raw source archives and private research evidence are intentionally kept outside the public tree.
